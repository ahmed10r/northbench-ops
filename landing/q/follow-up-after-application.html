How long to wait before following up on a job application

Applications rarely die from a bad CV. They die in the silence after submitting - and the follow-up email is the only lever you have on a process that forgot you exist.

The short answer

  • Wait five to seven working days after applying, then send exactly one follow-up.
  • Reply to the application confirmation if you have it; otherwise write a fresh short email to the hiring address or the recruiter named in the post.
  • Three sentences: what you applied for, one line on why you fit, one clear question about the timeline. That is the whole email.
  • Follow up once. A second follow-up more than doubles the annoyance and adds almost nothing to your odds.
  • Track it: application date, follow-up date, and any answer. What is not tracked does not get followed up at all.

The email itself

  1. Subject: 'Following up - [role title] application'. Do not write 'checking in' - name the thing.
  2. Line one: 'I applied for the [role] on [date] and wanted to confirm my application is with you.'
  3. Line two: one sentence on fit, specific to the job post - the number they asked for, the tool they named. Not a paragraph about you.
  4. Line three: 'Is there an updated timeline for interviews?' - a question they can answer in one line.
  5. Send it Tuesday to Thursday, working morning their time. Monday mornings bury it; Friday afternoons forget it.

What the silence means

Most silence is not rejection - it is overload. A posting for one role collects hundreds of applications and someone has to read them between their actual job. Your follow-up does two quiet jobs: it confirms you are still available and interested, and it moves your name to the top of the pile one more time. Neither is manipulation; both are the normal mechanics of a busy process. What silence almost never means is a verdict about your worth - and treating it as one is how people stop applying in week three, which is the one sure way to never get hired.

  • Two weeks of silence after one follow-up: keep applying elsewhere, leave the door open.
  • A rejection after a follow-up is a real answer - thank them and move on same day.
  • An interview invite is not a job - your tracking sheet should have a stage column, not a hopes column.

Mistakes that cost interviews

  • Following up after two days - reads as anxiety, not interest.
  • Following up three times - at some point the recruiter starts avoiding your name on purpose.
  • Sending a different CV 'just in case' - now there are two of you in the pile and one is wrong.
  • Not tracking applications at all, so the same company gets two applications and zero follow-ups.

Questions people actually ask

Should I follow up after an interview too?

Yes, but that is a different email: a same-day thank you, two sentences, one specific thing from the conversation. Then wait for their stated timeline before anything else.

What if the posting says no calls or no follow-ups?

Believe it. The instruction is a filter, and the follow-up then costs you the application you already paid for.

Does following up actually help?

It does what it can: confirms availability and re-surfaces your name. It will not fix a weak fit - and it does not need to, because its job is only to stop you being forgotten, not to argue your case.

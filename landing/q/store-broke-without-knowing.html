How to know your online store broke before your buyers do

Stores do not break loudly. A listing goes unpublished, a file fails to attach, a price gets fat-fingered - and everything looks fine from the inside, because no dashboard emails you about any of it.

The short answer

  • Check five things on every listing, every day: is it published, is the price what you meant, is the file attached, does the cover load, do the links on the page still land.
  • A human will not do this daily for thirty listings. It is boring, it is repetitive, and the day you skip is the day it breaks. This is a job for a script, not for willpower.
  • The check has to read the store the way a buyer reaches it - the public page - not the inside of your dashboard, because the buyer never sees your dashboard.
  • Compare today against yesterday, and say only what moved. A daily report that lists everything is a report nobody reads by Thursday.
  • Worst thing first. An unpublished listing costs money every hour; a changed cover picture can wait until lunch.

The four ways stores break silently

  • The unpublish: a toggle slips, a draft is saved over a live listing, a platform migration flips a switch. The buy page returns a 404 and every link you ever posted now leads nowhere.
  • The missing file: the listing is live, the price is right, and the download is empty. The buyer pays, gets nothing, and asks for a refund - or worse, does not ask and never comes back.
  • The price typo: 19 becomes 1.9, or 199. One sells at a loss, the other stops selling at all. Nobody emails you either way.
  • The quiet rot: a cover image host dies, an external link rots, a refund policy line gets deleted in an edit. Each one alone is small; together they read as neglect to a first-time buyer.

Make the check run itself

  1. Pick the pages that matter: the storefront, each listing, and the one or two landing pages you actually send people to.
  2. Put a small script on a schedule - cron on a Linux box or a Mac, Task Scheduler on Windows, or a free GitHub Action. One line, runs once a day.
  3. The script fetches each page the way a browser does, pulls out the fields a buyer would see, and saves today's snapshot.
  4. The next day it compares, and writes one short letter: what changed, worst first, each line pointing at a page and a moment.
  5. Read the letter with your morning coffee. Ninety days out of a hundred it says nothing moved. The tenth day is the one that pays for the other ninety.

What monitoring cannot tell you

  • Visits, sales and search rank live behind logins. A public-page watcher cannot see them, and a tool that claims otherwise is guessing.
  • Nobody can watch your store more honestly than the page itself. If the buy button is on the public page, it gets checked; if it is behind a login, it does not, and the report should say so.
  • Monitoring is not the same as fixing. It tells you the listing is unpublished by 9am; you still have to click the button.

Questions people actually ask

Is once a day enough?

For a small store, yes. Platforms already watch their own uptime; what they do not tell you is when your own listing quietly stops being for sale. Once a day catches that within a day, which is about a hundred times faster than most sellers find out.

Do I need to give a monitoring tool my store password?

No, and you should not. Everything worth checking - published state, price, files, cover, links - is on the public page. A tool that reads only public pages has nothing to leak.

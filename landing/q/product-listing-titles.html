How to write a product title that people actually find

Most listings are named like a project and searched for like a need. The gap between the two is the whole difference in being found.

The short answer

  • Say what it is in the buyer's words, then who it is for: 'Freelance invoice tracker for one-person studios'.
  • Put the format in the title: spreadsheet, template, PDF, pack, checklist. Buyers search for formats.
  • Keep the title under about 70 characters so nothing gets cut off in a list.
  • The first line of the description repeats the promise in plain words, because that is what a search result shows.
  • Write the price line next, so nobody has to hunt for it.

A formula you can fill in

  1. Write the need as a search a stranger would type. Not your product name - the problem.
  2. Take the four or five words from that search that describe what it is.
  3. Add the format: template, spreadsheet, checklist, pack.
  4. Add the audience if it narrows usefully: freelancers, landlords, students, small shops.
  5. Read it out loud. If it sounds like a slogan, rewrite it until it sounds like a description.
  6. Do the same for the first description line, then the second. Two honest lines beat a paragraph of adjectives.

Before you publish

Know the price and why it is that price. A listing that cannot answer 'why this number' gets discounted on the spot, and a discount invented in a hurry is the beginning of a bad reputation.

Look at your own listing as a search result: title, first line, price. If those three do not answer what it is, who it is for and what it costs, the rest of the page is decoration.

  • No invented reviews, counts or badges. Ever - it is the fastest way to lose an account.
  • Say what is not included. It prevents the refund and the bad feeling.
  • Use the words a buyer would say out loud, not the words you use internally.

Where this goes wrong

  • Naming a product after yourself or a codename. Nobody searches for either.
  • Stacking keywords until the title reads like a machine wrote it - search engines and humans both dislike it.
  • Hiding the price at the bottom of a long description.
  • Copying a competitor's title exactly. It puts you second in a race you already lost.

Questions people actually ask

How long should a description be?

As long as it needs to answer: what it is, what is inside, the format, the price, and what is not included. For most files that is three to six short sections.

Do tags matter?

They help a marketplace's own search, so use the phrases a buyer would type, and keep them honest - tags that do not describe the file mostly waste the space.

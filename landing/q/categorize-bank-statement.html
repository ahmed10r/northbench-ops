How to categorize a bank statement without losing a weekend

Categorizing spending fails when every row needs a decision. The fix is a small fixed set of categories, rules that handle the obvious rows, and a question list for the rest - not a 40-category spreadsheet you abandon in week two.

The short answer

  • Export your statement as CSV. Every bank has it; if yours hides it, the app support page knows where.
  • Keep three columns: date, description, amount. Everything else is noise for this job.
  • Start with eight to ten categories, not forty: housing, food, transport, subscriptions, utilities, health, fun, work, other. Renaming is cheap; maintaining a taxonomy is not.
  • Categorize by vendor, not by row. One rule - 'this supermarket is food' - clears hundreds of rows at once.
  • Collect the rows you cannot name into a question list instead of guessing. A category called 'maybe' is honest; a wrong category quietly corrupts every total after it.

The method, step by step

  1. Open the CSV next to a plain table with your category names across the top. Paper works; a spreadsheet works; a markdown file works.
  2. First pass: vendors you recognize instantly. Supermarket, landlord, phone company. This is usually half the rows and takes minutes.
  3. Second pass: recurring amounts on the same date each month. Those are subscriptions - mark them all the same and note them separately, because that list is worth money on its own.
  4. Third pass: the question list. Anything ambiguous gets its date, vendor and amount written down, and you answer the whole list at once with fresh eyes - not row by row.
  5. Total each category and stop. Do not budget yet. The first honest total is the whole win; the plan is a separate job that goes better with real numbers in hand.

What most people get wrong

  • Too many categories. If a category needs explaining to your future self, it will be empty by March.
  • Guessing ambiguous rows to 'finish'. One guessed row is a rounding error; fifty quietly change the answer.
  • Categorizing every month from scratch. The second month should reuse the vendor rules from the first - that is the entire reason to keep them.
  • Mixing the categorizing with the judging. Total first, feelings second. The numbers survive the feelings; not the reverse.

Questions people actually ask

Should I use an app that connects to my bank instead?

If you are comfortable giving a third party read access to your account, they do the categorizing for you. This page is for people who are not - the CSV export keeps your numbers on your machine and no credential ever leaves your hand.

How long should the first month take?

With a fixed category set and vendor rules: twenty to thirty minutes the first time, under ten after that. If it takes much longer, the category list is too complicated.

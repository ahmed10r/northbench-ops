How do I ask a parent to pay for tutoring lessons?

The money conversation is the one tutors lose by improvising - usually by not having it. The fix is arithmetic first, then a short message, then a policy that makes the next conversation unnecessary.

Why it got awkward

  • The balance grew one lesson at a time, and each lesson felt too small to mention. Thirty dollars is a moment; two hundred is a confrontation.
  • You are billing a person you also see at the kitchen table every week. The relationship and the invoice share a room.
  • You never set a payment moment, so payment became a thing that happens when someone remembers - and the parent's memory has no fee column.
  • Nothing was written down, so both of you are honestly guessing. Guessing makes everyone defensive.

The three-sentence message

  1. Sentence one, the fact: 'Quick update - through the end of October, Layla has had twelve sessions, and the total is 216 dollars, of which 54 is still open.'
  2. Sentence two, the door: 'Happy to keep the usual rhythm - bank transfer or cash, whatever is easier.'
  3. Sentence three, the quiet close: 'I'll send a short total at the end of each month so it never piles up.'
  4. Send it the same day you write it. Delay is where the message curdles into an apology you did not owe.

The policy that prevents round two

  • Pick a payment rhythm and say it at sign-up: per session, weekly, or monthly. Any of the three works; 'eventually' does not.
  • Decide the cancellation rule now, in writing: 24 hours' notice or the session is charged at half, or whatever is true for you - the number matters less than its existence.
  • Send the monthly total on the same date every month, before it is awkward rather than after. A predictable invoice is admin; a surprise one is a grievance.
  • Apply every rule to every family. The exceptions you make for the nice ones are the ones that cost the most.

What not to do

Know the exact number before you write. 'About fifty' invites a debate; '54' ends it.

  • Do not apologize for the existence of the fee. You are not requesting a favour; you are closing a ledger.
  • Do not soften the number by making it fuzzy. 'A few sessions' reads as negotiable; 'twelve sessions, 54 open' reads as done.
  • Do not wait for the term to end. Balances collected in-month stay small; balances collected at Christmas end friendships.
  • Do not make it a conversation about the child's progress at the same time. One topic, one message; praise has its own envelope.

Questions people actually ask

What if the parent disputes the count?

This is what a same-day session log is for: dates and minutes you can both read. A log built after the dispute starts is an argument; one kept all along is a receipt.

Should I stop lessons over an unpaid balance?

Rarely, and never as a surprise. A stated policy - a pause after two unpaid months, for example - applied evenly is fair; an improvised freeze in a bad week is a scene.

What to post when you have nothing to say

The blank feeling is not a lack of ideas. It is the belief that a post must be an idea. Your week already contains a dozen posts if you know where to look.

The short answer

  • Post the work, not thoughts about the work. A photo of the thing you made today beats an opinion about the industry.
  • Post the question you genuinely have - real questions get answered, fake ones get ignored, and the answers are your next three posts.
  • Post the decision: what you chose this week and why. People collect other people's decisions; it is the cheapest useful content there is.
  • Post the number: how long it took, what it cost, what it earned. Numbers are content and honesty is rare enough to stand out.
  • Keep a fallback list of ten half-written posts. Blank days are for the list, not for brilliance.

The five-minute unblanking

  1. Open your week: sent emails, finished tasks, screenshots, questions you asked anyone. Everything there is a candidate.
  2. Pick the one with a concrete detail - a number, a name of a tool, a before and after. Concrete survives; abstract dies.
  3. Write it as three sentences: what happened, what surprised you, what you would do differently. No lessons unless one actually occurred.
  4. Cut the first sentence. It was almost certainly a warm-up.
  5. Post it. The account that posts average things weekly beats the account that posts perfect things never.

Why the blank feeling lies

Blank-days panic comes from imagining the audience as a crowd waiting to be impressed. The actual audience is a few people scrolling, half-attention, looking for something recognizable. They do not need your best idea; they need a true sentence they can nod at. This is also why the calendar method works: the decision of what to post today was made weeks ago on a calm day, and today you only execute. The blank feeling is a decision-making problem wearing a creativity costume - take the decision away and the blank goes with it.

  • Perfect is a postponement strategy that feels like quality.
  • The archive compounds: a year of honest small posts is a body of work; five brilliant ones are a screenshot.
  • Silence has costs too - the audience that forgot you exist does not come back to check.

Posts you can write today

  • Something you fixed this week and the one tool or step that mattered.
  • A price or a timeframe, said plainly - the post most people are afraid to write.
  • A question your customers ask you twice a week, answered once, properly.
  • A mistake you made and what it cost - the highest-trust post per minute of writing there is.

Questions people actually ask

How often should I post when starting from zero?

Once or twice a week, sustained for months, beats daily for two weeks followed by silence. Pick the rhythm you can keep on your worst week - that is the real constraint.

Should I repost old content?

Yes - if it was true and useful, most of the audience never saw it. Repost with light edits, not apologies.

What about trending topics?

Only when you have something specific and true to add. Chasing trends with nothing to say trains the audience that you have nothing to say.

What records should a private tutor keep?

You do not need a filing system. You need four lists: who you teach, what happened, what was agreed, and what was paid. Everything else a tutor archives is decoration.

The four lists

  • Students: name, subject, rate, who pays (student or parent), and status. This is your register and your price list in one.
  • Sessions: date, minutes, topic, homework. Written same-day, this list becomes your progress evidence, your billing source and your memory.
  • Agreements: the rate as agreed, the cancellation policy as shown at sign-up. When a dispute starts, you want a dated line, not a recollection.
  • Money: what was charged, what was paid, what is still owed, per student. The unpaid column is the one that pays for the record-keeping.

What to skip

  • CRM features. You have twelve students, not twelve thousand; a pipeline stage called 'discovery call' is a costume.
  • Detailed lesson plans archived forever. The topic and the homework line are enough; the plan lived its life during the lesson.
  • Time-tracking apps. The session row already holds the minutes; a second tool measuring the same hour is a subscription with no job.
  • Anything that needs a login to answer 'what does this family owe me'.

The tax angle, briefly

  • Your session log is your income evidence: dates, amounts, totals. Most tax systems want exactly that shape.
  • Keep the payment records (transfers, receipts) beside the log, not inside your head or your chat history.
  • Expenses count too: materials, travel, the platform fees. A simple 'money out' list beside the 'money in' one is usually enough for a sole tutor.
  • This is record-keeping, not tax advice - your local rules decide the details, your lists make them answerable.

Making it survive the term

  1. Fix one weekly slot for admin and defend it like a lesson slot - it pays like one.
  2. Write the session row before you close the laptop, while the homework you set is still true.
  3. Once a month, read the summary out loud: sessions, income, unpaid. Numbers you say become numbers you act on.
  4. At term end, archive a copy and start the next term's file clean. A year in one file is history; a term is a tool.

Questions people actually ask

How long should I keep tutoring records?

Long enough for your tax authority and any dispute window - commonly several years. Since it is one small file, keeping it costs nothing.

Should records live with the parent or with me?

Yours. You can share a progress summary whenever asked, but the billing and session history is your business's memory, and it should live in your house.

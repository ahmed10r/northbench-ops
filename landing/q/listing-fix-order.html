What to fix first when a product listing gets no attention

A listing is read in one order: the title, the first line, the picture, the price. Fix in that order, and you stop paying for a photoshoot to solve a title problem.

The repair order

  1. Title: does it contain the words a buyer would type, in the order they would type them?
  2. First line: does it say what the thing is and who it is for, in one sentence, before any adjectives?
  3. Main photo: can a stranger tell what is being sold at thumbnail size, on a phone, with the sound off?
  4. Price: is it derived from what the thing costs you to make plus what the market charges, or from a feeling?
  5. Description: does it answer the four questions - what is inside, what format, how it arrives, what it does not include?
  6. Then, and only then, look at tags, banner images and the wording polish.

One change a week

Change everything at once and you learn nothing, because you cannot tell which change did the work. Change one line a week, write down the date beside it, and after a month you will have a short list of what your buyers actually respond to.

A week is long enough for a quiet listing and too short for a busy one, so use two weeks where the traffic is heavy. Judge by views and saves, not by how much you like the new line.

  • Keep the old version of every line you change. A change is only an improvement if you can compare it with what came before.
  • Change the thing the buyer reads first, not the thing you enjoy editing.
  • If two changes in a row make no difference, the problem is probably further up the list.

The price line deserves arithmetic

A price set by feeling usually means one of two things: the work is being subsidised without anyone deciding to subsidise it, or the listing sits above its market for no stated reason. Work out your floor first - what the item costs you in materials, time and fees - and treat anything above that floor as a choice you can explain out loud.

The free calculator takes your own cost numbers and returns a price that covers them. Nothing leaves the page, so you can test three versions before you touch the listing.

  • Write the floor down before you look at competitors, so their price does not become your floor.
  • If your current price is below the floor, either raise it or stop selling that item. Both are honest.
  • Recheck the floor when materials, fees or platform rules change. Fee changes happen quietly.

What to ignore for now

  • Rewriting the whole description before the title has been fixed.
  • Buying ads to send strangers to a listing whose first line explains nothing.
  • Copying a competitor's tags without knowing what they were aiming at.
  • Redesigning the entire store before a single listing has been read properly.

Questions people actually ask

How long before I judge a change?

A week for a quiet listing, longer for anything seasonal, and always against the same measure you used before the change. Otherwise you are comparing moods.

Should the title be a proper sentence?

No. It is a search line first and a sentence second. Say the thing clearly, because cleverness has nothing to index.

The event planning timeline: what to settle when

Events rarely fail from lack of effort. They fail from doing the right jobs in the wrong order - booking the caterer before knowing the headcount, printing before the final list exists.

Eight weeks out: the three anchors

  • The date and the venue, together, because each one constrains the other.
  • The budget as a number, not a mood - and the first split: venue, food, everything else.
  • The guest count as an estimate with an honest range, because every later price depends on it.

Four weeks out: the list becomes real

  1. Send the invitations with a reply-by date three weeks before the event, and say what happens after that date.
  2. Start the tracking list: invited, replied, coming, declined, no answer yet - one list, updated in one place.
  3. Book food against the estimate, not the final count, and confirm the final count later as the contract allows.
  4. Confirm anything with a lead time: rentals, cake, printing, the person doing the music.

Two weeks out: the shape of the day

  • Chase the no-answers; a reply you chased is worth two you assumed.
  • Draft the seating while the list can still move, so the final version is an edit and not a rebuild.
  • Write the running order for the day itself: arrivals, food, speeches, the end. Half a page, in time blocks.
  • Confirm every vendor in writing, one message each, with the times and the numbers repeated back.

The last week: only two jobs belong here

  • The final headcount to the food people, on the day their contract asks for it.
  • The final seating and the final running order, printed or on a phone that works offline.
  • Everything else was supposed to be done. A last week with a long list means an earlier week had a short one.
  • Keep the receipts list going as you pay. The honest total, after the event, is next year's budget's best friend.

Questions people actually ask

What if I have less than eight weeks?

Compress the order, never skip it: anchors first, list second, shape of the day third, confirmations last. The reason the order exists is that each step prices the next one - skip it and you pay for the same things twice.

What can safely be decided late?

Table decorations, the music order, the favors - anything with no lead time and no dependency. What cannot wait is anything another person needs in order to do their part.

What every invoice line item should say (and what never to write)

An invoice is not a bill, it's a claim that has to survive someone else's approval process. Line items written for the approver - specific, verifiable, boring - get paid on the first pass.

The short answer

  • One line per deliverable or per day, never both mixed. Pick the unit the client recognizes from the scope note.
  • Name the thing, not the effort: 'Homepage redesign - second revision' not 'various design work'.
  • Reference the agreement: a scope-note number or date in the reference column ends most 'what is this for' emails.
  • Dates on every line. An approver who can't place the work in time can't approve it quickly.
  • The one line that prevents disputes: 'Includes: the two rounds of revision agreed in the scope note. Additional rounds billed at [rate].' Written once, on every invoice.

The line, word for word

  1. Start with the noun the client uses: their name for the project, not yours. 'Acme site - homepage redesign' beats 'design services'.
  2. Add the stage: first draft, revision, final. Stages show progress and make partial invoices self-explanatory.
  3. Add the quantity in the client's unit: days, pages, pieces, hours - whatever the scope note priced. Mismatched units are the classic query trigger.
  4. The rate goes on the line, not just the total. Approvers with budgets approve faster when the math is visible.
  5. Group by project when a month has several, with one subtotal per project. Six flat lines across three projects reads like a mystery; three grouped sections read like a report.

Words that trigger queries

  • 'Miscellaneous' - a query, every time. If it's real work, it has a name.
  • 'As discussed' - discussable, therefore discussed. Put what was discussed in the line instead.
  • 'Admin' - replace with the actual activity: 'project coordination and file management, Oct 1-15'.
  • Round-day padding - invoice the honest 0.5. Approvers notice suspicious roundness, and trust is the currency that clears invoices fast.

Questions people actually ask

Should I include the hours if I billed a flat fee?

No - flat fee means the deliverable is the line, and hours invite re-derivation of your price. Show the deliverable, the stage and the fee; the math visibility rule is for time-based billing.

How much detail is too much?

The approver should understand every line in five seconds without asking you anything. If a line needs a follow-up email to explain, it was two lines pretending to be one.

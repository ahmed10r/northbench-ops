How to test an AI prompt pack before you trust it

Prompt packs are sold by their table of contents and their word count, which are the two things that say least about them. The only useful test is your own work.

The three-prompt test

  1. Pick a real task you finished last month and still have the output of.
  2. Choose three prompts from the pack that claim to do that task, or the closest thing in it.
  3. Run all three on the same real input, and keep every result.
  4. Compare what comes back with what you produced by hand. Not with each other.
  5. Ask one question of each result: would I send this after one edit, or start again?

What a good prompt entry includes

  • The variables marked clearly, so you know exactly what to swap.
  • A note about when it does not apply.
  • The shape of the output: a table, a list, an email, a short paragraph.
  • Enough context that the result is about your case rather than about the world in general.
  • One line explaining why it is written that way, so you can adjust it.

Where packs go wrong

Two failures account for most disappointment. The first is prompts written for a demonstration rather than a job: they produce impressive text about a topic instead of a draft you can use. The second is a pack that is one idea repeated sixty times with the nouns changed.

Both are easy to spot before you pay. Read three entries from the middle of the pack rather than the first three, because the opening is always the strongest, and check whether the instructions differ in substance rather than in wording.

  • A pack that never says when not to use a prompt is selling enthusiasm.
  • Volume is not quality: sixty variants of one instruction still answer one task.
  • If the pack offers a free sample, test the sample before you look at the price.

After the test

  • Keep the prompts that survived in the file where you keep your own notes.
  • Rewrite the wording in your own vocabulary. Prompts written for everyone are written for no one.
  • Write down what you changed, so the improvement can be repeated next time.
  • Delete the rest. A pack you have half-tested is a folder you will browse instead of working.

Questions people actually ask

Is a free sample enough to judge a pack?

Usually yes, if the sample shows working prompts rather than teasers. Three complete examples make the method visible, and the method is what you are actually buying.

Do longer prompts work better?

Longer is not better; clearer is. Length helps only when it carries context you would otherwise repeat every single time.

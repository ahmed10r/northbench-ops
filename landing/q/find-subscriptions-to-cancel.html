How to find every subscription you forgot you had

Subscription audits pay for themselves the same evening, because the average person pays for three to five things they no longer use. The method is boring: find the recurring charges, total them per month, ask each one three questions.

The short answer

  • Search your statement for the same amount repeating on a monthly cycle - same number, roughly same date, several months in a row.
  • Check the amounts people forget: small ones under ten, and the yearly ones that land as a surprise once a year.
  • Total the monthly equivalent of everything recurring. That one number is usually the motivator.
  • Cancel by writing the cancellation date down, not by clicking the button and hoping - yearly resurrections are a business model now.
  • Re-run the audit every quarter. Subscriptions breed when nobody is watching.

The audit, in one evening

  1. Gather the last three months of statements. Three months is enough to see a monthly cycle twice and catch the yearly ones you forgot.
  2. Mark every repeating charge: same vendor or recognizably same service, same or similar amount, roughly monthly gap. Streaming, software, hosting, gym, apps, news, cloud storage.
  3. Write the list: vendor, amount, cycle, what it was for when you signed up. If you cannot remember what it is for, that is your first cancellation candidate.
  4. Ask each one the three questions: did I use it this month; would I buy it again today if I did not have it; is there a cheaper tier that covers what I actually use.
  5. Cancel the losers and write the date next to each one. Watch the next statement for charges that survived their own cancellation - they are common enough to have a name now: ghosts.

The three questions, honestly

  • Did I use it this month - actual use, not intended use. Intent is how the subscription got there in the first place.
  • Would I buy it again today - this kills the sunk-cost defense better than any argument.
  • Is the cheaper tier enough - sometimes the answer is downgrade, not cancel.

Questions people actually ask

Is this worth doing if I only have a few subscriptions?

A few subscriptions at typical prices is often twenty to sixty dollars a month. The audit takes one evening. Few legal activities pay that hourly rate.

What about subscriptions on a card I rarely use?

Those are the most important ones - rarely-used cards are where forgotten charges live longest. The audit covers statements, not cards: pull them all.

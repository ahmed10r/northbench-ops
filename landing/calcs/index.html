the shelf / calculators

Free calculators

8 of them. Each takes what you type and does the sum. The workbook underneath is the part that remembers your numbers next month.

  • Invoice total, with a discount and a tax rate
    Type the line totals, the discount you agreed, and the tax rate that applies to you. It adds up. That is all it claims to do.
  • What an hour has to cost to pay you
    Start from the money you need, then work back to a rate. Billable hours are the honest part: it is the hours you can actually charge for, not the hours you sit at a desk.
  • A project price from the work you actually listed
    Task by task, then a buffer, then expenses. A fixed price with no buffer is a promise with nothing behind it.
  • What a store actually leaves you
    A price is not a payout. Percentage fees, fixed fees and a higher-traffic cut stack on top of each other, so the sticker number is never the arriving one.
  • A price that covers the cost and leaves something
    A margin is a share of the price, not a mark-up on the cost. Mixing those two up is the most common way a small seller works for free.
  • The 50 / 30 / 20 split, on your own numbers
    Needs, wants, saving - and what is left after the bills that do not move. It is a starting shape, not a rule.
  • How long a goal takes at a set amount
    Goal, what you put aside, how often. It divides. It will not tell you that you will keep to it.
  • A bundle price that does not undercut yourself
    Add the parts, set the discount, and see what a buyer pays versus what you would have made selling them apart.
Why they are free: a page that is genuinely useful is the only honest way to be found. Nothing here collects you, and nothing here forecasts your income.

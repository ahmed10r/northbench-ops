The tutor tracker: how to organize a tutoring practice without an app

A tutoring business fails administratively before it fails academically. The parents who owe you money, the student you double-booked, the session you forgot to log - none of those are teaching problems. They are list problems, and lists are a solved technology.

The four lists that run everything

Every tutoring practice, from three students to thirty, runs on four lists. If they live in your head, you will lose money in proportion to how busy you get.

  • Students - name, parent contact, rate, subject, standing note. The standing note is the one that matters: "confident but freezes at exams", "will cancel Thursdays in Ramadan." Teaching memory is professional capital.
  • Sessions - date, student, duration, rate, paid or not. The unlogged session is the unpaid session; it is that simple.
  • Payments - what was paid, when, against which sessions. Parents are honest but busy; the invoice that arrives clearly, with dates, gets paid before the vague one.
  • The schedule - recurring slots with the student's name on them, so double-booking becomes visually impossible instead of socially awkward.

The two numbers parents never argue with

At the end of every month you should be able to say two sentences: "X sessions at $Y is $Z" and "you have paid $A, so $B remains." Numbers with dates behind them end discussions. Numbers without dates start them.

Why a spreadsheet beats an app here

Tutoring software charges monthly, holds your data hostage, and is designed for schools, not for one person with eleven students and a conscience. A spreadsheet you own does the arithmetic forever, for free, and your student list is never one expired subscription away from gone.

The system in practice

Log sessions the same evening - two minutes. Invoice monthly with dates attached - ten minutes. Keep one standing note per student - ongoing. That is the entire operational load of a well-run tutoring practice, and it is why tutors who use a system like Tutor OS stop losing the 5-10% of revenue that unlogged sessions and vague invoices quietly eat.

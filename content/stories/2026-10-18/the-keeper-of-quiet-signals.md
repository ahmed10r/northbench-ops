# The Keeper of Quiet Signals
*Northbench Studio - Fiction drop #1 (written by the deep agent)*

The observatory on the hill had been closed for eleven years when Mira
found the tape recorder.

It sat on the desk of the last astronomer, under a inch of dust,
still plugged in, still warm. That was the first strange thing. The
second was the label, in careful pencil: *SIGNALS NO ONE ANSWERED,
VOLS. 1-40.*

She pressed play.

Static. Then a voice, tired and kind: "Log for March the third. The
dish heard something tonight at 1420 megahertz. Fourteen minutes,
then silence. Probably the amplifier. Probably." A pause. "But I
stayed an extra hour anyway. You understand why, or you wouldn't be
listening to tape forty."

Mira played the rest of the reel that night, cross-legged on the
floor of a dead man's office. Vol. 12: a burst from the direction of
a star with no name, "like someone clearing their throat before
speaking." Vol. 23: seventeen identical pulses, "which is either a
malfunction or patience, and I no longer know which one I'm hoping
for." Vol. 31: nothing at all, and the voice saying, "Logged
nothing. Still logging. That's the job."

The last tape was different. The voice was old. "They shut the
place down Friday. I asked to keep the recorder. They said the
observatory is done listening. I didn't correct them." A long
breath. "If you're hearing this, the machine is still warm because
someone kept it warm, and the sky is still talking because it
doesn't know how to stop. Signals don't need an answer to be real.
Neither does listening."

Mira sat in the dark for a while. Then she did what the job now
was: she found a fresh tape, wrote *VOL. 41* on it in careful
pencil, and pressed record.

Outside, the dish on the hill turned, slow as a flower, toward the
part of the sky where the morning was still being decided.

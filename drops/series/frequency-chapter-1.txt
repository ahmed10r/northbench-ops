THE FREQUENCY - CHAPTER ONE: 3:07

Maya's radio had two rules: it only turned on at 3:07 AM, and it
never played the same song twice.

She found the second rule out on night four, when she requested
her mother's favorite - the station's only listener-host had
laughed softly and said, "You know I can't do that, kid. The
Frequency doesn't repeat. Not for anyone. Not even for grief."

The host called himself The Operator. He sounded sixteen, same
as her. He took requests for songs about things people missed:
old houses, dead dogs, brothers who'd moved away and called
less and less. Then he'd play something that fit exactly, like
he'd been saving it for years.

On night nine, Maya keyed the mic and asked the question
everyone in town under twenty eventually asked.

"Operator, who ARE you?"

Static. Then: "Tune in tomorrow, 3:07. I'll tell you the truth
if you promise not to tell the adults."

She promised.

At 3:06 AM the next night, every clock in her house stopped.
